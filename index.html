01 — What you own

Private. Deployable. Yours.

Three questions decide whether a firm can use AI at all: where the material sits, who runs the model, and whether it knows the work of the house. Most vendors answer the third by taking the first two away from you.

Radice answers all three without the file leaving the building.

Control

The data never leaves

Packs, matters, mail and the working papers stay in the room they are already in. No third-party API, no shared tenancy, and no copy of the corpus anywhere we can reach.

Deployment

Your machines, or your tenant

On hardware the practice already owns, on-premise in the office, or a European tenant in the client's own name. Their keys, their bill, their jurisdiction.

Adaptation

Trained on your own material

Small models tuned to the document types the firm actually handles, so an answer arrives in the language of the house and points at the page it came from.

02 — The premise

The model goes to the file.

Everything useful a firm knows is already written down — in packs, in mail, in the working papers of people who have left. The reason nobody can use it is not intelligence. It is that the material cannot be sent anywhere without breaking the obligation the firm is paid to keep.

So we invert the shape. The boundary is drawn once, in hardware the firm already owns or a European room in its own name, and the work happens inside it. That constraint is not a compromise we tolerate. It is the product.
