06 — Company

Sovereignty is an engineering problem.

Radice is a small engineering firm. We put working intelligence inside boundaries other people already own, and then we leave it there. Everything else about how we are built follows from that one decision.

The argument

Nobody is asking for less intelligence.

Every practice we sit down with has the same two facts stacked on top of each other. Almost everything the firm knows is already written down — in packs, in mail, in the working papers of people who left years ago. And none of it can be sent anywhere, because not sending it is precisely what the firm is paid for.

The industry's answer has been to ask firms to relax the second fact for a while. Ours is to move the model instead of the material. It is the harder engineering problem and a far shorter conversation with a partner.

01

The constraint is the product, not a compromise we tolerate.

02

A small model on your material beats a large one on somebody else's.

03

If it cannot point at the page, it should decline to answer.

The order

Accountancy first. Then law. Then the state.

We are deliberately not everywhere at once. Accountancy practices come first because the document types repeat, the calendar is brutal and legible, and a partner can tell inside a fortnight whether the thing works. Law firms follow, since the shape is identical and the stakes make the boundary argument without our help.

Public bodies move on their own timetable, which is longer than ours. We keep the door open rather than pretending to have walked through it.

Now

Accountancy practices in Europe. The close, the pack, the letter that has to sound like the firm that sent it.

Next

Law firms. The same visit and the same stack, with matter files where the client books were.

On their timetable

Authorities and public bodies. A written scope before anything is installed, and a process rather than a signature.

Not yet

Anything that needs a model we cannot run inside a boundary the client controls. We will say so rather than stretch the definition.

The firm

Small, and staying that way.

There is no sales floor between you and the people who will be standing in your office. The person who writes the brief on day fourteen is the person who builds the kit afterwards.

That is a constraint as much as a virtue. We take few engagements at a time, and we would rather tell you when the next slot actually is than book you into one that does not exist.

No shared tenancy

We will not put many firms' material into one model that we operate. There is no version of that which keeps the promise.

No borrowed credibility

No client logos we have not earned, no case studies from firms that did not agree to be named, no time-saved percentage we have not measured.

No certification theatre

We claim no ISO, SOC or chamber membership, because we hold none yet. When we do, the certificate number will be on this site.

No lock-in

A runbook ships with the kit so the practice can keep it without us. Leaving should cost a conversation and nothing else.
